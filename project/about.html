What "MS-65" actually means

Grading is just a shared vocabulary for describing wear and eye appeal, so a buyer in another state can trust a listing without holding the coin. CoinBest uses the same 70-point Sheldon scale that most US dealers and grading services use.

The grading scale, plain-language

Code Name What to look for
P-1 / FR-2 Poor / Fair Barely identifiable; date or design may be worn away entirely.
G-4 Good Heavily worn; major design elements are flat but outlines are visible.
VG-8 Very Good Design is worn but complete; some detail remains in the deepest areas.
F-12 – F-15 Fine Moderate, even wear; major details are worn but clear.
VF-20 – VF-35 Very Fine Light wear on the highest points only; most fine detail is sharp.
XF-40 – XF-45 Extremely Fine Slight wear on the highest points; design is crisp overall.
AU-50 – AU-58 About Uncirculated Trace of wear visible only under close light; most original luster remains.
MS-60 – MS-70 Mint State No wear at all; grade within this range depends on strike, marks, and luster.

A short history of coined money

Knowing roughly when a coin's design or minting method originated helps explain why some pieces are common and others are scarce.

  • ~600 BC Electrum coins are struck in the kingdom of Lydia, in present-day western Turkey — widely considered the first true coinage.
  • 211 BC Rome standardizes the silver denarius, which circulates across the empire for centuries and forms the backbone of most ancient coin collections today.
  • 1792 The United States Mint is established in Philadelphia, beginning a domestic coinage that collectors now organize by mint mark and design type.
  • 1878 The Morgan silver dollar begins production, becoming one of the most widely collected US coin series due to its long run and large surviving mintages.
  • 1986 The US Mint introduces the American Gold and Silver Eagle bullion program, giving collectors a way to hold precious metal in a recognized, tradeable coin.

Before you buy or sell

  • Handle Hold coins by the edge, over a soft surface, to avoid adding fingerprints or scratches that lower the grade.
  • Compare Check a coin's price against recent sales of the same date, mint mark, and grade — not just the same coin type.
  • Ask A seller should always be able to explain why a coin is graded the way it is. If they can't, ask for more photos before you commit.
